One approach to create a report

My colleague Aisha Umair has graciously created the outline below, and I took the liberty to make some modifications.

The Report Outline

  • Front page
    Should at least include:
    • Project type
    • Title on the project
    • Name of student(s) and SDU mail(s).
    • Month and year
    • Supervisor names
  • Abstract
    Should outline the project by addressing the topic, motivation, aim, methodology, results, and conclusion.
  • Acknowledgements (if any)
  • Contents
  • List of Figures
  • List of Tables
  • List of Listings (pseudo-codes, algorithms…)
  • Acronyms (if any)
  • Glossary
    An alphabetical list of project-specific terms along with their definitions helps readers grasp unfamiliar or specialized terminology used in the report.
  • Introduction
    In this section, you could write about:
    • Context
    • Motivation
    • Problem
    • Scope
    • Objectives
    • Expected contributions, etc...
  • Background/Related Work
    This section should examine similar work on the same topic found in the literature. Additionally, it may be beneficial to include a segment on relevant concepts that could assist the reader with your project.
  • Existing System (if any)
    In this section, you should present the current system if your project is based on or an extension of it. Topics to include are:
    • Architecture
    • Implementation, etc...
  • Proposed System
    Here you could add:
    • Requirements (functional, non functional)...
    • You could also do Requirement prioritization (MoSCoW)
    • Design diagrams (UML, sequence diagrams, flow diagrams)
  • Implementation
    Here you could add:
    • How you implemented your system.
    • May include code snippets for front end/ backend
  • Testing/Experiments and Results
    Here, you consider all the possible cases and scenarios for testing experiments and create a separate section for each case to evaluate the efficacy of your proposed methodology.
  • Evaluation/Discussion
    Here you could discuss about the
    • Shortcomings in your projects and
    • How could it be mitigated and
    • Your reflections on the results.
  • Conclusion and Future Work
    Here you could discuss about the
    • Summarize your work and
    • Write how this work could be extended in future
  • References
    IEEE format: One approach to apply the format: IEEE Referencing Style Sheet.pdf.
  • Appendix (if any)
    • A,
    • etc...

Code Snippets

Please carefully consider why your are including your code snippets in your report. Often classical software engineering diagrams provides a lot more value.

Number of pages???

☝️ The quality of your content and the concise delivery of your messages always outweigh the number of pages in your report. Always.
  • BSc. projects based on a group with two students:
    • Typically 40 "normal pages"
    • 15 to 20 fewer or more "normal pages" for groups consisting of one or three students, respectively.
  • BEng. projects based on a group with two students:
    • Typically 50-60 "normal pages"
    • 20 to 25 fewer or more "normal pages" for groups consisting of one or three students, respectively.

A "normal page" is defined as having 2400 characters, incl. spaces and excl. figures, tables, etc. - Reference: SI-Generelt-Form og Aflevering.pdf
